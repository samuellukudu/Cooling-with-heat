Vehicle waste-heat cooling — 120 °C exhaust becomes 7 °C cabin air

Application vehicle: T_evap 7 °C · T_cond 45 °C · T_des 120 °C · half-cycle 180 s · fluid 90–130 °C · SCP weight 0.40

1 · The plumbing (this truck only)

Heat in: exhaust gas (~320 °C at cruise) hands heat to a coolant loop through a gas/liquid exchanger. That loop (slider: idle 95 °C → cruise 125 °C) drives the bed to 120 °C.

Cooling out: the evaporator at 7 °C dehumidifies + cools cabin air (27 °C → 15 °C supply). Rejected: everything dumps at 45 °C to ram/hot ambient air — the harshest lift in the set (7 → 45 °C), which is why COP is structurally capped here.

⚠ Honesty flag, kept live below: every measured isotherm in the table tops out well below 120 °C (MOF-74-Ni to 70 °C, MIL-160 to 40 °C). Vehicle numbers are extrapolations, shown with the flag on.

3 · Compact bed under vibration — live cells

14 cells, 1.5 mm, k = 0.03 s⁻¹. Adsorb: wall 45 °C at p_evap(7 °C) = 1.00 kPa. Desorb: wall = loop slider at p_cond(45 °C) = 9.59 kPa. High back-pressure is the enemy here — watch q* refuse to empty unless the loop is truly hot.
