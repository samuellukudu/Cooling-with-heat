Wearable TGC — body heat → electricity via thermogalvanic redox

From intro-research: thermogalvanic cells generate electricity through thermoelectrochemical reactions of redox couples. Flexible, textile-compatible, Carnot-relative efficiency up to 8%.

1 · The cell

A gel-electrolyte thermogalvanic cell laminated into fabric. Two electrodes at different temperatures drive a redox reaction: the temperature difference shifts the equilibrium potential (Seebeck coefficient of the redox couple, S_redox). Current flows through an external load. Key design: prevent electrolyte leakage (gel matrix), maximise electrode surface area (textile electrodes).

Carnot-relative efficiency up to 8% (ultraflexible quasi-solid TGCs). Power density lower than solid TEGs but flexible, stretchable, and washable.

2 · The textile cell

Canvas: skin → textile electrode → gel electrolyte → textile electrode → ambient. Redox reaction at each electrode; temperature difference drives the potential.
