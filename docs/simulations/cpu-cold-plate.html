CPU / electronics cold-plate assist — where 75 °C heat becomes cooling

Application cpu: T_evap 18 °C · T_cond 35 °C · T_des 75 °C · half-cycle 120 s · fluid 60–85 °C · SCP weight 0.45 (power density case)

1 · The plumbing (this application only)

Heat out of the chip: a 65 W CPU dumps to a water cold plate (~22 °C return). That water is the cooling load — it is chilled in the evaporator at 18 °C saturation (p ≈ 2.06 kPa), boiling the refrigerant.

Heat into the machine: a separate 60–85 °C loop (rack exhaust recovery, slider below) heats the bed to 75 °C to desorb vapour. The vapour condenses at 35 °C (dry-cooler reject), throttles to the evaporator, and the twin bed — cooled back to 35 °C — adsorbs it, sustaining suction on the evaporator. That is the whole trick: high-grade heat makes low pressure, low pressure makes cold.

Heat rejected: adsorption heat + condensation heat leave at 35 °C to the dry cooler. Energy balance always: Q_rej = Q_in + Q_cool.

2 · Live heat flows — widths ∝ power

Equilibrium cycle (Magnus psat + Watson h_fg + D–A uptake, hx = 1.35 metal penalty). T_des follows the source slider — starve the source toward 60 °C and watch Δq and COP sag.

3 · The bed itself — live finite-difference simulation (not a replay)

This canvas integrates a real (toy) transient model in JS, specific to this app: 14 cells across a 1.2 mm silica/MOF coating, FTCS conduction (α = 3.2e-7 m²/s), convective wall to the HTF fluid, and LDF uptake dq/dt = k·(q*−q) with k = 0.05 s⁻¹ (fast — this app punishes slow kinetics). Adsorb half: wall = 35 °C, vapour = p_evap(18 °C). Desorb half: wall = source slider, vapour = p_cond(35 °C). Cell colour = temperature, cyan dots = adsorbed water. 120 s halves — watch the thermal wave cross in seconds.
