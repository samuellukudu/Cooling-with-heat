Data-center warm-water loop — why 60 °C regeneration breaks zeolite 13X

Application datacenter: T_evap 16 °C · T_cond 35 °C · T_des 60 °C · half-cycle 300 s · fluid 45–70 °C · the hard low-grade case

1 · The plumbing (this hall only)

Heat in: server cold plates dump to a warm-water loop at 45–70 °C (slider). That loop — barely warmer than the 35 °C reject — must regenerate the bed at just 60 °C.

Cooling out: the evaporator at 16 °C makes chilled water for CRAH units (safe above dew point, no magnets condensing). Rejected: adsorption + condensation heat at 35 °C to the dry cooler.

The head-line finding (H2.3) lives here: zeolite 13X — the textbook adsorbent — ranks 19/21 because its D–A working capacity collapses at 60 °C. Toggle the material and watch Δq die.
